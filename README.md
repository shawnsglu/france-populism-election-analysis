# Presidential Coattails and the Electoral Performance of Populist Parties

## Overview

This project examines how presidential elections affect the legislative performance of populist parties.

Drawing on the median voter theorem and principal-agent theory, I argue that presidential candidates from populist parties face incentives to moderate their policy positions in order to attract broader electoral support. While this strategy may improve their chances of winning executive office, it can create an ideological gap between presidential candidates and their parties, weakening the coattail effect that traditionally benefits legislative candidates.

Using electoral data from France's 2017 and 2022 general elections, as well as evidence from Brazil's 2022 general election, this project explores how institutional arrangements shape the electoral fortunes of populist parties.

---

## Research Question

**How do presidential elections affect the legislative performance of populist parties?**

More specifically:

* Do populist presidential candidates moderate their positions to appeal to median voters?
* Does this moderation reduce support for legislative candidates from the same party?
* How do presidential systems create incentives that differ from parliamentary systems?

---

## Theoretical Argument

This project combines insights from:

* Median Voter Theorem
* Principal-Agent Theory
* Literature on Presidential Coattail Effects
* Research on Populist Parties

I argue that populist parties face a unique dilemma in presidential systems.

To maximize their chances of winning executive office, presidential candidates often move toward the political center. However, this strategic moderation can create tensions with party organizations and core supporters, resulting in weaker coattail effects during legislative elections.

Presidential systems provide populist parties with an opportunity to capture executive power without first establishing a large parliamentary presence. While this creates opportunities for electoral success, it also generates internal accountability challenges when party leaders and presidential candidates pursue different electoral strategies.

## Main Findings

The results suggest that populist parties face structural disadvantages arising from presidential coattail dynamics.

Evidence from France indicates that:

* Presidential candidates from populist parties often adopt more moderate positions than their parties.
* This moderation can weaken the connection between presidential and legislative voting.
* Voters are more likely to split their tickets when they perceive differences between presidential candidates and their party organizations.
* Presidential institutions create incentives that may benefit individual candidates while simultaneously reducing legislative gains for their parties.
